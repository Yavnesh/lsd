# Knowledge Universe Architecture

## The Core Concept
The Knowledge Universe transforms traditional learning categories into a spatial, exploratory map. Users navigate this universe to uncover knowledge, battling the "Fog of War."

## Hierarchy
1. **Knowledge Worlds (Macro)**
   - Massive domains of learning. (e.g., *The Psychological Realm*, *The Silicon Galaxy*).
   - Worlds are visually distinct biomes on the Knowledge Atlas.
2. **Regions (Meso)**
   - Specific sub-domains within a World. (e.g., *Cognitive Biases*, *Memory Systems*).
3. **Topics (Micro)**
   - The individual nodes on the map. (e.g., *The Dunning-Kruger Effect*).
   - Completing a Topic clears adjacent Fog of War.
4. **Missions / Quests**
   - The actual learning interaction (currently "Lessons"). These are the challenges users complete to conquer a Topic.
5. **Mastery & Discoveries**
   - Scoring 100% or completing all sub-quests in a Region yields a **Relic** or **Artifact**.
   - Hidden nodes (Discoveries) only appear under specific conditions (e.g., completing 3 related nodes quickly, or playing on a specific real-world holiday).

## Discovery Mechanics (Fog of War)
When a user begins, they only see a fraction of the Knowledge Universe. 
- **Adjacent Unlocks:** Mastering a node reveals the names/paths of directly connected nodes.
- **Wormholes / Secret Links:** Some topics connect across different Worlds (e.g., a Psychology node linking to an Economics node).
- **Anticipation:** Users should frequently encounter locked nodes labeled "Unknown Phenomena", driving curiosity.

## Scalability
This architecture scales infinitely. 
- 10 Worlds can hold 100 Regions.
- 100 Regions can hold 1,000 Topics.
- Adding new content doesn't clutter a list; it expands the physical edges of the Universe.
